Flight Log Book

Overview​

The Flight Log Book application is a comprehensive tool for managing and recording all aspects of aircraft flight operations in Maximo for Aviation. This application serves as the central hub for documenting flight activities, tracking aircraft status, managing crew assignments, and recording maintenance-related information discovered during flight operations.

The Flight Log Book provides aviation maintenance and operations personnel with a complete view of:

  • Flight data including crew assignments, fuel replenishments, and munitions usage
  • Scheduled and completed flights
  • Real-time aircraft status and meter readings
  • Inspection schedules and maintenance requirements
  • Discrepancies identified during flight operations
  • Deferred maintenance items
  • Equipment inspection lists and history

The Flight Log Book integrates with other Maximo for Aviation applications to provide a seamless workflow from flight planning through maintenance execution.

Key Features:

  • Comprehensive flight data recording and management
  • Real-time aircraft status monitoring
  • Integration with preventive maintenance scheduling
  • Discrepancy reporting and tracking
  • Deferral management capabilities
  • Equipment inspection tracking

Flight Data​

The Flight Data section captures all essential information related to individual flights, including crew assignments, fuel and oil replenishments, and munitions usage. This section provides a complete record of each flight operation and serves as the foundation for operational reporting and analysis.

Flight data is typically entered either:

  • Manually by operations or maintenance personnel after flight completion
  • Automatically through integration with flight data systems
  • Via mobile applications used by flight crew

Accurate flight data entry is critical for maintaining compliance with regulatory requirements and ensuring proper aircraft maintenance scheduling.

Flight Crew​

The Flight Crew section allows you to assign and document all crew members associated with a specific flight. This includes pilots, co-pilots, flight engineers, and any other crew positions required for the mission.

Crew Assignment Process:

  1. Navigate to the Flight Crew tab within the Flight Data section
  2. Click Add Row to add a new crew member
  3. Select the crew member from the person lookup
  4. Assign the appropriate crew position (e.g., Pilot, Co-Pilot, Flight Engineer)
  5. Enter flight hours for the crew member (if different from total flight time)
  6. Save the crew assignment

Crew Position Types:

  • Pilot in Command (PIC) - Primary pilot responsible for the flight
  • Co-Pilot - Second pilot assisting with flight operations
  • Flight Engineer - Crew member responsible for aircraft systems monitoring
  • Navigator - Crew member responsible for navigation duties
  • Loadmaster - Crew member responsible for cargo and weight distribution
  • Additional Crew - Other crew positions as required by mission type

Crew assignments are used for tracking individual flight hours, currency requirements, and qualification maintenance.

Best Practices:

  • Verify crew qualifications before assigning to flights
  • Ensure crew duty time limitations are not exceeded
  • Document any crew changes or substitutions
  • Maintain accurate flight hour records for each crew member

Replenishments​

The Replenishments section tracks all fuel, oil, and other consumable items added to the aircraft before, during, or after flight operations. This information is critical for:

  • Fuel consumption analysis
  • Cost tracking and allocation
  • Maintenance planning (oil consumption trends)
  • Operational efficiency monitoring

Recording Replenishments:

  1. Navigate to the Replenishments tab
  2. Click Add Row to create a new replenishment record
  3. Select the replenishment type (Fuel, Oil, Hydraulic Fluid, etc.)
  4. Enter the quantity added
  5. Specify the unit of measure (gallons, liters, quarts, etc.)
  6. Record the location where replenishment occurred
  7. Enter the date and time of replenishment
  8. Save the record

Common Replenishment Types:

Engine OilLubricating oil for enginesQuarts, Liters
Hydraulic FluidHydraulic system fluidQuarts, Liters
OxygenBreathing oxygen for crewPounds, Liters
CoolantEngine cooling fluidQuarts, Liters

Tracking oil consumption trends can help identify potential engine issues before they become critical failures.

Fuel Consumption Analysis:

  • Compare fuel loaded vs. fuel remaining to calculate consumption
  • Track consumption rates per flight hour
  • Identify anomalies that may indicate fuel system issues
  • Support mission planning with accurate fuel requirement estimates

Munitions​

The Munitions section documents all weapons, ordnance, and related equipment loaded, expended, or removed during flight operations. This section is primarily used for military aviation operations and provides critical tracking for:

  • Weapons inventory management
  • Mission effectiveness reporting
  • Safety and compliance documentation
  • Maintenance requirements triggered by weapons usage

Munitions Tracking:

  1. Navigate to the Munitions tab
  2. Click Add Row to add a munitions record
  3. Select the munitions type from the catalog
  4. Enter the quantity loaded
  5. Record the quantity expended during the flight
  6. Document the quantity returned/remaining
  7. Specify the station or hardpoint location
  8. Add any relevant notes or comments
  9. Save the record

Munitions usage may trigger specific maintenance inspections or requirements. Ensure all expended munitions are properly documented.

Maintenance Implications:

  • Certain weapons may require post-firing inspections
  • High-G maneuvers during weapons employment may trigger structural inspections
  • Gun firing may require barrel inspections after specific round counts
  • Weapons bay door cycles may affect maintenance schedules

Flight Schedule​

The Flight Schedule section provides visibility into planned and scheduled flights for aircraft in your fleet. This section helps operations and maintenance personnel coordinate activities, plan resources, and ensure aircraft availability for scheduled missions.

Schedule Management Features:

  • View upcoming flights by aircraft, date range, or mission type
  • Identify scheduling conflicts or aircraft availability issues
  • Coordinate maintenance activities around flight schedules
  • Track schedule adherence and on-time performance
  • Plan crew assignments and resource allocation

The Flight Schedule integrates with the Flight Log Book to automatically create flight records when scheduled flights are completed.

Schedule Views:

  • Daily View - Shows all flights scheduled for a specific day
  • Weekly View - Provides a week-at-a-glance schedule
  • Aircraft View - Displays schedule for a specific aircraft
  • Mission Type View - Groups flights by mission category

Planned Flights​

The Planned Flights section displays detailed information about upcoming flights, including departure and arrival times, locations, crew assignments, and mission requirements. This information is used to:

  • Ensure aircraft are properly configured for planned missions
  • Verify crew availability and qualifications
  • Coordinate maintenance activities to avoid schedule conflicts
  • Plan fuel, munitions, and other resource requirements

Viewing Planned Flights:

  1. Navigate to the Flight Schedule section
  2. Select the Planned Flights tab
  3. Use filters to narrow the display by:
    • Date range
    • Aircraft
    • Mission type
    • Status (Scheduled, Confirmed, Cancelled)
  4. Click on a flight to view detailed information

Flight Status Indicators:

  • Scheduled - Flight is planned but not yet confirmed
  • Confirmed - Flight is confirmed and resources are allocated
  • In Progress - Flight is currently underway
  • Completed - Flight has been completed and logged
  • Cancelled - Flight has been cancelled
  • Delayed - Flight departure has been delayed

Regularly reviewing planned flights helps identify potential conflicts between maintenance requirements and operational schedules.

Coordination with Maintenance:

  • Check aircraft status before confirming flights
  • Identify maintenance windows between scheduled flights
  • Coordinate urgent maintenance with operations to minimize schedule impact
  • Update flight schedules when maintenance delays occur

Aircraft Status​

The Aircraft Status section provides a comprehensive, real-time view of the aircraft's current condition, including meter readings, inspection schedules, engine data, and maintenance holds. This section is essential for:

  • Determining aircraft readiness for flight operations
  • Monitoring maintenance requirements and due dates
  • Tracking component life limits and time-change items
  • Identifying issues that may affect aircraft availability

Status Overview Features:

  • Current meter readings (flight hours, cycles, calendar time)
  • Upcoming inspection requirements
  • Engine and APU operating parameters
  • Active maintenance holds and restrictions
  • Component status and life remaining

The Aircraft Status section is automatically updated when flight data is entered or maintenance activities are completed.

Status Indicators:

  • Green - Aircraft is fully mission capable
  • Yellow - Aircraft has minor restrictions or upcoming maintenance
  • Red - Aircraft is not mission capable or has critical issues
  • Blue - Aircraft is in scheduled maintenance

Flight Meter Data​

The Flight Meter Data section displays current readings for all meters associated with the aircraft. Meters track various usage parameters that drive maintenance requirements, including:

  • Flight hours (total time the aircraft has been airborne)
  • Flight cycles (number of takeoff/landing sequences)
  • Calendar time (elapsed time since manufacture or overhaul)
  • Engine hours (operating time for each engine)
  • APU hours (auxiliary power unit operating time)

Viewing Meter Data:

  1. Navigate to the Aircraft Status section
  2. Select the Flight Meter Data tab
  3. Review current meter readings and last update dates
  4. Compare current readings to maintenance thresholds
  5. Identify meters approaching maintenance limits

Meter Information Display:

ColumnDescription
Meter NameDescription of the meter (e.g., Flight Hours, Cycles)
Current ReadingMost recent meter value
Last UpdateDate and time of last meter reading
Average RateAverage accumulation rate (per day, per flight, etc.)
Next InspectionNext maintenance requirement based on this meter
RemainingTime/cycles remaining until next maintenance

Meter readings must be accurate and up-to-date to ensure maintenance is performed at the correct intervals. Verify meter readings after each flight.

Meter Types:

  • Continuous Meters - Accumulate continuously (e.g., flight hours)
  • Gauge Meters - Show current state (e.g., fuel quantity)
  • Characteristic Meters - Track specific conditions (e.g., temperature)
  • Delta Meters - Calculate differences between readings

Best Practices:

  • Update meter readings immediately after each flight
  • Verify meter accuracy by comparing with aircraft systems
  • Investigate significant deviations from expected accumulation rates
  • Ensure meter readings are synchronized across all systems

  • Electrical power when main engines are not running
  • Pneumatic power for engine starting
  • Air conditioning and pressurization on the ground
  • Backup power for essential systems

APU Information:

  1. Navigate to the APU History tab
  2. Review APU operating hours and cycles
  3. View maintenance history and upcoming requirements
  4. Monitor performance trends and parameters
  5. Access component-level information

APU Maintenance Considerations:

  • APU maintenance intervals are typically based on operating hours
  • Start cycles can significantly impact component life
  • Monitor oil consumption trends for early problem detection
  • Coordinate APU maintenance with aircraft maintenance schedules
  • Ensure APU is operational before extended ground operations

Common APU Issues:

  • Electrical or pneumatic system anomalies

Hold Item List​

The Hold Item List displays all active maintenance holds, restrictions, and limitations currently applied to the aircraft. Holds prevent the aircraft from being released for flight until specific conditions are met or maintenance actions are completed.

Types of Holds:

  • Safety Holds - Critical safety issues that ground the aircraft
  • Maintenance Holds - Scheduled or unscheduled maintenance in progress
  • Inspection Holds - Required inspections not yet completed
  • Configuration Holds - Aircraft configuration does not meet requirements
  • Administrative Holds - Documentation or regulatory compliance issues
  • Operational Holds - Operational restrictions or limitations

Viewing Hold Items:

  1. Navigate to the Hold Item List tab
  2. Review all active holds for the aircraft
  3. Click on a hold to view detailed information
  4. Identify actions required to clear each hold
  5. Monitor hold status and clearance progress

Hold Information Display:

Aircraft with active safety holds must not be released for flight under any circumstances until the hold is properly cleared by authorized personnel.

Hold Management Process:

  1. Hold Application - Hold is placed on aircraft when issue is identified
  2. Work Assignment - Maintenance work order is created to address issue
  3. Work Completion - Required maintenance or inspection is completed
  4. Verification - Work is inspected and verified by qualified personnel
  5. Hold Clearance - Hold is removed and aircraft is released for flight

Best Practices:

  • Review hold items before every flight release
  • Ensure all required documentation is complete before clearing holds
  • Verify that corrective actions actually resolve the issue
  • Maintain clear communication between operations and maintenance
  • Document all hold clearances with appropriate signatures and dates

The Aircraft Inspection and Maintenance section provides tools for documenting and managing maintenance activities discovered during flight operations or routine inspections. This section is critical for:

  • Recording discrepancies found during pre-flight, in-flight, or post-flight inspections
  • Creating maintenance work orders to address identified issues
  • Tracking the status of maintenance actions
  • Ensuring all discrepancies are properly resolved before flight release

Discrepancies entered in the Flight Log Book are automatically integrated with the Maximo work management system for tracking and resolution.

Inspection Types:

  • Pre-flight Inspection - Conducted before each flight
  • Through-flight Inspection - Conducted between flights
  • Post-flight Inspection - Conducted after flight completion
  • Daily Inspection - Conducted once per day
  • Special Inspection - Conducted for specific purposes

Discrepancies​

The Discrepancies section allows flight crew, maintenance personnel, and inspectors to document any issues, defects, or abnormalities discovered during aircraft operations or inspections. Proper discrepancy reporting is essential for:

  • Maintaining aircraft safety and airworthiness
  • Ensuring regulatory compliance
  • Tracking maintenance trends and recurring issues
  • Providing accurate maintenance history

Creating a Discrepancy:

  1. Navigate to the Discrepancies tab
  2. Click Add Row to create a new discrepancy
  3. Enter a clear, detailed description of the issue
  4. Select the affected system or component
  5. Specify the severity level (Critical, Major, Minor)
  6. Indicate when the issue was discovered
  7. Add any relevant photos or documentation
  8. Save the discrepancy

Severity Levels:

Critical discrepancies must be addressed immediately and may require notification to regulatory authorities depending on the nature of the issue.

Discrepancy Workflow:

  1. Reporting - Discrepancy is documented in the Flight Log Book
  2. Review - Maintenance control reviews and prioritizes the issue
  3. Work Order Creation - Work order is generated to address the discrepancy
  4. Maintenance Action - Technicians perform required repairs or inspections
  5. Verification - Work is inspected and verified for quality
  6. Closure - Discrepancy is closed and aircraft is released

Best Practices for Discrepancy Reporting:

  • Be specific and detailed in descriptions
  • Include exact locations (e.g., "Left main landing gear, forward trunnion")
  • Document what was observed, not assumptions about the cause
  • Include relevant operating conditions when the issue occurred
  • Attach photos or diagrams when helpful
  • Reference applicable technical manuals or procedures
  • Update status as work progresses

Common Discrepancy Categories:

  • Structural - Cracks, corrosion, damage to airframe
  • Systems - Hydraulic, electrical, pneumatic system issues
  • Powerplant - Engine or APU problems
  • Avionics - Navigation, communication, or instrument issues
  • Flight Controls - Issues with control surfaces or systems
  • Landing Gear - Gear, brakes, tires, or related components
  • Environmental - Pressurization, air conditioning, oxygen systems

Deferred Maintenance​

The Deferred Maintenance section manages maintenance items that have been identified but are allowed to be deferred under specific conditions. Deferral programs allow aircraft to continue operations with certain non-critical items inoperative, provided specific safety and operational requirements are met.

Deferral Programs:

  • Minimum Equipment List (MEL) - Defines equipment that can be inoperative
  • Configuration Deviation List (CDL) - Allows operation with missing components
  • Master Minimum Equipment List (MMEL) - Regulatory basis for MEL
  • Operational Limitations - Restrictions applied when items are deferred

Deferred maintenance items must be tracked carefully to ensure they are resolved within required timeframes and that operational limitations are observed.

Deferral Categories:

  • Category A - Must be repaired within specified time (usually 24 hours)
  • Category B - Must be repaired within 3 consecutive calendar days
  • Category C - Must be repaired within 10 consecutive calendar days
  • Category D - Must be repaired within 120 consecutive calendar days

Deferrals​

The Deferrals section provides detailed tracking of all deferred maintenance items, including:

  • Description of the inoperative item or system
  • MEL or CDL reference
  • Deferral category and expiration date
  • Operational limitations or restrictions
  • Placarding requirements
  • Maintenance actions required for clearance

Creating a Deferral:

  1. Navigate to the Deferrals tab
  2. Click Add Row to create a new deferral
  3. Select the MEL or CDL item from the reference list
  4. Enter the discrepancy or reason for deferral
  5. Specify the deferral category
  6. Calculate and enter the expiration date
  7. Document any operational limitations
  8. Specify required placarding
  9. Save the deferral

Deferral Management Process:

  1. Identification - Inoperative item is identified during operations or maintenance
  2. MEL Review - Maintenance control reviews MEL to determine if deferral is allowed
  3. Deferral Application - Deferral is documented with appropriate category and limitations
  4. Placarding - Required placards are installed on the aircraft
  5. Operations Notification - Flight crew is briefed on limitations
  6. Monitoring - Deferral expiration is tracked
  7. Resolution - Maintenance is performed before expiration
  8. Clearance - Deferral is cleared and placards are removed

Operating an aircraft beyond a deferral expiration date is a serious violation of regulations and may result in enforcement action.

Operational Limitations Examples:

  • Maximum altitude restrictions
  • Speed limitations
  • Weather minimums (VFR only, day only, etc.)
  • Passenger capacity restrictions
  • Route or area restrictions
  • Equipment requirements (e.g., must have specific backup systems)

Best Practices:

  • Review all active deferrals before each flight
  • Ensure flight crew is aware of all limitations
  • Monitor deferral expiration dates closely
  • Plan maintenance to clear deferrals before expiration
  • Verify placards are properly installed and visible
  • Document all deferral-related communications
  • Consider cumulative effects of multiple deferrals

Deferral Clearance:

  1. Perform required maintenance to restore item to service
  2. Conduct operational checks to verify proper function
  3. Remove all placards related to the deferral
  4. Update deferral status to "Cleared"
  5. Document clearance with appropriate signatures
  6. Notify operations that limitation is removed
